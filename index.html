Lesson 1 · about 20 minutes

Set up your phone's camera

Your iPhone makes a lot of choices on its own. Most of them suit holiday snaps, not opal. These settings stop it changing colour, brightness and lens while you film. You only do this once.

Check your phone first: open Settings, tap General, then About. Next to iOS Version it should say 27 or higher.

If it says a lower number, stop here and message Andre. He'll help you update first.

Before you change anything

Film your "before" video

  1. Pick one stone you like, and remember where you're sitting.
  2. Film it for about 10 seconds the way you normally would, under your usual lights, rocking it slowly.
  3. Send the video to Andre on Messenger.

Don't try to make it perfect. You'll film the same stone again at the end of this lesson, and again at the end of the course.

The pictures below come from other iPhones, so your screens may look a little different. Look for the same words.

  1. Sharper video

    Settings›Camera›Record Video
    4K at 30 fpsTap to choose

    Keeps fine detail in close-ups, so the pattern stays crisp after Facebook shrinks the video.

  2. Turn off HDR video

    Settings›Camera›Record Video
    HDR VideoOFF

    Facebook doesn't show HDR video reliably. Colours can come out washed-out or glaring.

  3. Stop the lens switching

    Settings›Camera›Record Video
    Lock CameraON

    Your phone has three lenses. This stops it jumping between them in the middle of a video.

  4. Keep colours steady

    Settings›Camera›Record Video
    Lock White BalanceON

    When the fire flashes, the phone tries to "correct" it and the whole stone changes colour. This stops that.

  5. Choose a photo style

    Settings›Camera›Photographic Styles
    StandardStart here

    Styles change the colour and contrast of every photo you take (not your videos). Standard adds nothing, so it's the honest starting point. Here's roughly what the others do to opal:

    Rich Contrast
    Deeper shadows, punchier colour. Black opal can look darker than it really is, and detail in dark areas gets lost.
    Vibrant
    Brighter, stronger colours. Fire can look more intense than it does in the hand.
    Warm
    A golden tint. Boulder ironstone looks rich, but blues and greens shift, and crystal turns yellowish.
    Cool
    A blue tint. Blues and greens look clean, but red fire weakens and a white or crystal body looks blue.

    Have a play

    1. Open the Camera, then tap the round button with six dots at the top right. (Swiping up from the words Photo and Video does the same.)
    2. Tap Styles, then swipe left and right through them.
    3. Take one photo of the same stone in each style.
    4. Hold the stone next to the phone and look at the photos. Which one looks most like the real stone?

    Go back to Standard when you're done. If another style matches the stone better, tell Andre which one; it might suit some of your stones.

  6. Show the grid

    Settings›Camera
    GridON

    Faint lines on the screen help you keep the stone centred and straight. They don't appear in your photos.

  7. Add the close-up lens switch

    Settings›Camera
    Macro ControlON

    When you hold the phone close to something, it swaps to a special close-up lens on its own. This setting adds a small flower button, so you can see when that happens and choose for yourself.

  8. Make the phone remember

    Settings›Camera›Preserve Settings
    Exposure AdjustmentON
    Macro ControlOFF

    The camera keeps the brightness you set. Leaving Macro Control off here means the close-up lens always starts on automatic, so nothing you changed last time catches you out.

  9. Meet the flower button

    In the Camera, just to try it

    1. Open the Camera.
    2. Hold the phone about a hand's width from a small object, like a stone or a cup, until a yellow flower button appears at the bottom left of the picture. Yellow means the close-up lens is on.
    3. Tap the flower. It goes grey: you're back on the normal lens.
    4. Tap it again to turn it yellow.
    5. Close the Camera and open it again. It's back on automatic.

    Yellow flower means the close-up lens, grey means the normal lens. In Lesson 3 you'll learn when to use each one, and how to choose before you start filming.

Your practice task

Film your "after" video

  1. Same stone, same spot, same lights as your "before" video.
  2. Film it again for about 10 seconds, rocking it slowly.
  3. Send it to Andre on Messenger.

The difference may be small. These settings mostly stop the colour and lens jumping during a video. The big change comes in Lesson 2, with your new light.

If something looks different on your phone

Take a screenshot by pressing the side button and the volume up button at the same time. Send it to Andre on Messenger and skip that step for now.
